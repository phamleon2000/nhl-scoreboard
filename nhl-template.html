Over/under

Our total points against the Vegas total, with every factor and each team's over/under trend added up.

How this works

Our total is a weighted mix: Vegas total 40%, home/away model 20%, standard model 10%, OddsShark 10%, DRatings 10%, Dimers 10% and play-by-play efficiency (EPA, pulled halfway back toward average) 10%, rescaled over the sources a game has. Both models already include kickoff weather, indoor/outdoor and rain.

Then the over/under trend: how often each team's games went over the Vegas total this season (3+ games each). When the two teams together went over (or under) 65%+ of the time, the total moves up to 1 point that way.

The pick is Over when our total is above the Vegas total, Under when below. Strong = 3+ points away, Lean = 1.5–3, Slight = under 1.5. Finished games use only what was known before kickoff. Pace is seconds per snap in close games (lower = faster, more plays).

Final predictions

Every final pick is against the live sportsbook spread (not the moneyline), with all factors added up, and graded on the closing line.

How this works

Every factor counts. Picks and results go by the live sportsbook line (Maddux Sports, the middle of six books; the closing line once a game starts), or the Vegas consensus when there are no live odds for a game. The predicted margin is a weighted mix of: that market line 40%, home/away model 20%, standard model 10%, power ratings 10% and Dimers 20% and the Fanatics Markets prediction market 10% (weights rescale when a source is missing). Both models already include key-player injuries, kickoff weather, indoor/outdoor and rain. Then small nudges: spread money movement ±0.5 pt (±1 when strong) and Covers users' spread picks ±0.5 when 60%+ agree, and the day/night trend for the game's kickoff slot, up to ±1.5 pts on the margin and ±2 on the total (see Day vs night games on the Results tab). Moneylines and straight-up winner picks are not used. The total is Vegas 50%, home/away model 30%, standard model 20%.

The final pick is the side our final margin beats the Vegas spread on. Strength: Strong when it beats the line by 3+ points, Lean 1.5–3, Slight under 1.5. Cover chance uses the same 13.5-point spread of outcomes. Tap "How every factor adds up" on a game card to see each source's share.

1st & 2nd half picks

Our side and over/under for each half, against the sportsbooks' half lines.

How this works

Each half is built the same way as the final pick: a weighted mix of the same sources at the same weights, then the same nudges. The sources: the books' half line 40%; our half model (each team's scoring and allowing in that half) 20%; the standard model 10% and Dimers 20%, scaled to the half's usual share of the game (learned from this season's games); and power ratings 10% (1st-half power ratings for the 1st half). Totals add OddsShark, DRatings and EPA the same way. The money, Covers users and sharp-book nudges, and the over/under trend, are scaled to the half too. Tap "How it adds up" to see every piece. The deep matchup is shown as a check (✓ agrees, ✗ disagrees), as on the final pick.

At halftime the 2nd half updates: every 2nd-half source except the books' line moves by part of the 1st-half surprise (the real 1st-half score minus our 1st-half projection), in an amount learned from this season's finished games. The books' 2nd-half line, posted at halftime, already knows the score.

Lines are the middle of the books on Maddux Sports. Books post the 2nd-half line at halftime, so before kickoff the 2nd-half pick shows only our projection; it fills in on the live page once the line posts. The cover chance uses this season's own half-score misses. Strength: Strong 58%+, Lean 54%+, otherwise Slight. The 2nd half includes overtime, as at most sportsbooks. Break-even at −110 is 52.4%.

Home/away and standard picks

Who each of our two models picks to win, side by side.

How this works

Home/away uses the home team's home scoring and the visitor's road scoring. Standard uses each team's overall scoring plus a 1.5-point home edge. Both include passing & rushing, key-player injuries, weather and indoor/outdoor. "Spread" is how many points the model expects the winner to win by. When the two models disagree, the game is usually close.

Picks vs the Vegas line

Which side to take against the consensus spread, and over or under the total.

How this works

Four sources each say which side beats the spread: our home/away and standard models, Dimers and the money movement. A source counts when it beats the line by more than half a point.

  • Strong: 4 or more agree and our models beat the line by 3+ points.
  • Lean: more sources one way than the other.
  • Pass: no clear side.

Totals: our models' predicted points must be 2+ points over or under the line. Hover the Sources number to see each source. Break-even after the sportsbook's cut is about 52.4%.

Cover check · outside sites

Who each analysis site says covers the spread, next to our final pick. Spread only: no winners or totals.

How this works

OddsShark (computer picks) and DRatings publish a predicted score; the side whose predicted margin beats the Vegas line is their cover pick. nfelo (a model built on nflverse data) publishes its own spread line and marks the side with positive expected value. Pickswise is an expert's point-spread pick. Dimers and Covers users come from the existing prediction sources (Covers users are the public's spread picks). Fanatics is the Fanatics Markets prediction market: its fair line, worked out from the prices traders pay on a ladder of spreads, compared with the sportsbook line.

A site's pick is checked against the consensus line (or the Maddux Sports line when there's no consensus yet). A pick listed at a line more than 3 points away is ignored, since it's probably a different market. The record grades each site's last pre-kickoff pick on finished games against the spread; pushes don't count. Break-even after the sportsbook's cut is 52.4%.

Money trend · all betting sites

Where the bets (the public) and the money are going on the spread and the total, from Action Network, DraftKings, ScoresAndOdds, Sports Betting Dime and SBR.

How this works

Each site reports what share of bets (tickets) and what share of money (handle) is on each side. SBR reports bets only. The public side has 60%+ of bets on average. Sharp money is a side whose money share beats its bet share by 8+ points on average: fewer, bigger bets, which usually come from sharper bettors. A reverse line move is the line moving toward the side with under half the bets.

The money side feeds the final pick as a small nudge (half a point, or one point when strong). The record grades the money side on finished games against the spread.

Sportsbook analysis

What the individual sportsbooks' lines say: sharp books against public books, how far the line moved, the best number and the cheapest book.

How this works

Lines from six sportsbooks on Maddux Sports. Sharp books (Circa, Bookmaker, Pinnacle when listed) take big bets from professional bettors and usually move first; public books (DraftKings, Bovada) cater to casual bettors. When the sharp books' average spread is half a point or more different from the public books', the side the sharp books rate higher is the sharp lean, and it adds half a point to the final pick.

Move compares the opening line with the middle of the books now; crossing 3, 7 or 10 matters most because so many games end by those margins. Best number is the most points any book gives our pick's side. Cut is the bookmaker's built-in margin on the spread (lower is cheaper to bet). No-vig win % is the moneyline with that margin removed.

How to read the summary

Each finished game is graded exactly as an upcoming game would be, using only earlier weeks and the saved closing line at an assumed −110 (the real prices weren't saved). Only bets that passed both gates count as selections. Status: SUSPENDED when 10+ decided selections are clearly below the 52.4% break-even (a record that bad would happen less than 5% of the time if the market really broke even), VALIDATED FOR STATED SCOPE at 100+ selections with a profit, otherwise PROVISIONAL. A suspended market can't produce official picks until its record recovers. 1H markets stay UNTESTED because no past 1H lines are available. Brier score measures probability accuracy over every graded side (lower is better; 0.25 = coin-flip guessing).

Day vs night games

Does the time of day change the game? Scoring and betting results for day, twilight and night kickoffs this season. Each game card on the Games tab shows its slot.

How this works

Each game's sunset is worked out from the stadium's location and the date. Day: the game should end before sunset. Twilight: it starts in daylight and finishes under the lights. Night: it kicks off within 30 minutes of sunset or later. Outdoor and indoor stadiums are split: outdoors, daylight, glare, the lights and the evening chill can all play a part; under a roof only the time of day matters (body clock, the prime-time spotlight).

Overs and covers are graded on the saved closing line; pushes are left out. Our final pick is its record against the spread in that slot. Groups with few games swing a lot, so check the Games column.

In the model: for each slot (outdoor and indoor apart) the page learns, from earlier weeks only, how far its games' total points and the favorites' margin over the line differ from all games. Each is shrunk as if the slot had 20 extra average games, so a handful of games barely counts, and capped (total ±2 pts, spread ±1.5 pts). The final pick gets these as nudges (see "How every factor adds up" on a game card), and "Our read" gets a ✓ or ✗ line when the effect is half a point or more, which feeds the BET / SMALL BET / PASS decision. Power ratings & strength of schedule

How good each team really is once you account for who they've played, and how hard their schedule has been.

How this works

Rating is points better than an average team on a neutral field. Offense is points scored above average and Defense points kept off the board compared with average (higher is better for both). They're worked out together, so scoring 30 against a strong defense counts for more than 30 against a weak one, and each is shrunk a little because there are only a few games.

Schedule so far / left is the average rating of the opponents a team has played and still has to play (higher = harder; rank 1 = hardest).

Efficiency · EPA & success rate

Play-by-play efficiency for every team, best net EPA first.

How to read this

From nflverse play-by-play, the free data that RBSDM and Sharp Football Analysis build their team stats on. Only regular-season passes and runs count.

EPA/play (expected points added) is how much each play changed a team's expected points: higher is better on offense, lower is better on defense. Net is offense minus defense. Success is the share of plays that gained enough (40% of the yards needed on 1st down, 60% on 2nd, all of it on 3rd/4th). 1H EPA covers the 1st half only. Pass rate over expected is how much more (or less) a team throws than an average team would in the same situation (1st/2nd down, game still close). Pace is seconds between snaps when the score is within 7 in quarters 1–3 (lower = faster). Explosive is the share of plays with 20+ yard passes or 10+ yard runs. Red zone TD is the share of drives reaching the 20 that ended in a touchdown.

EPA is also a learned factor in our model ("Efficiency (EPA)" in How it works): it's only used if it would have made past predictions more accurate.

Ratings & betting trends · TeamRankings

TeamRankings' predictive rating, records against the spread and the total, and 1st-half scoring.

How to read this

Predictive rating is TeamRankings' points-better-than-average rating for future games. ATS is the record against the spread (wins-losses-pushes), Cover % how often the team covered and ATS +/− the average margin by which it beat (or missed) the spread. Over/under is how its games went against the total, and Total +/− the average points above the total. 1H scored / allowed are 1st-half points per game. ATS and over/under records swing a lot on small samples, so treat them as context, not predictions.

Luck check

Which teams' records are better or worse than their play, sorted luckiest first.

How this works

Should-have wins is the record a team's points scored and allowed usually produce (the "Pythagorean" formula). Luck is actual wins minus that: teams well above it often come back to earth. Close games is the record in games decided by 8 points or fewer, which tend to even out. Turnover margin is takeaways minus giveaways per game; big margins in either direction usually shrink later in the season.

Head coaches

How this works

From ESPN: each team's current head coach, how many seasons he has been an NFL head coach, and his record this season. A coach change is flagged when a coach's record covers fewer games than his team has played (he took over mid-season), or when the weekly update finds a different coach than last time. 1st-year coach means his first season as an NFL head coach; a coach who moved from another team isn't flagged, because ESPN doesn't keep past seasons' coaches.

Passing & rushing

Each team's style and yards per game, gained and allowed.

How this works

Style compares how often a team passes with the league average share of pass plays: 4+ points above is a passing team, 4+ below a rushing team, otherwise balanced.

In the model: each team's expected passing yards are the average of its passing offense and the opponent's pass defense, and the same for rushing. Those yards are turned into points at the league's points-per-yard rate. Yards are steadier than points, so the model moves part of the way toward this yards-based score. How far is learned from earlier weeks (at most halfway, so one bad stretch can't take over); it's 0 until there are enough games to measure.

Playoff chances

The rest of the season, played out 10,000 times.

How this works

Every remaining game is "played" 10,000 times using our win chances: the final prediction for this week's games (which includes Vegas) and the home/away model for later weeks. Each run counts each team's wins, crowns the 4 division winners in each conference (seeds 1–4 by record) and adds the 3 best other records as wild cards (seeds 5–7). Only the #1 seed gets a first-round bye.

Ties in the standings are broken by point difference so far, then at random. The NFL's real tiebreakers (head-to-head, division and conference record) aren't modeled, so close races can be off by a few percent.

How our models predict a score

  1. Team scoring: each team's expected points = the average of its offense and the opponent's defense. The home/away model uses home and road numbers, blended with overall averages because there are only a few games of each; the standard model uses overall averages plus a 1.5-point home edge.
  2. Learned factors: passing & rushing matchups, strength of schedule (opponent-adjusted ratings), rest & travel (byes, short weeks, miles, time zones, early body-clock kickoffs) and turnover luck. Each is only used if it improved past predictions; see "What the model has learned" above.
  3. Key injuries: a starting QB out is worth 5 points; other team leaders 1 (tackle leader 0.5).
  4. Weather: wind over 10 mph and freezing cold lower the total; indoor/outdoor and rain adjust it by how much they changed scoring this season.
  5. Win chance: assumes final margins land within about 13.5 points of the prediction either way.

The models ignore strength of schedule and injuries beyond team leaders. Treat every prediction as a rough guide, not betting advice.
